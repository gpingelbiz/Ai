Reel Editor

First time? Open Setup Guide.html for the setup walkthrough.

Drop a raw talking-head clip in a folder and get back a finished vertical reel: captions, tight cuts, a hook title placed around your face, a check on every word, and a ready-to-post caption.

You need: a Mac with Apple Silicon (M1 or newer) and macOS 14 or newer, about 4 GB of free space, and internet. The accounts that add the rest are listed right below, with links.

1. Install (20 to 40 minutes the first time)

  1. Double click Install Reel Editor.command in this folder. A Terminal window opens and does the work. Keep the whole kit together. The first install downloads about 2 GB.
  2. Answer these questions:
    QuestionWhat to pick
    Your lookThis kit carries your own brand, colors, caption voice and word list.
    ElevenLabs keyPaste it, or press Return to skip. Get one at elevenlabs.io, under Developers, API Keys.
    Drop folderPress Return for yes.
    CodexPress Return for yes if you have a ChatGPT account, then sign in in the browser window. It picks matching B-roll moments and adds generated carousel images.
    Free stock keysOptional: use your own Pixabay or Pexels key for free stock video. Links and filming instructions are in the B-roll section below.
    Runway keyPaste it, or press Return to skip. Make one at dev.runwayml.com (buy credits there first). It turns on Raw - Reel with Runway B-roll.
    ClaudePress Return for yes, then sign in with your Claude account (Pro or Max) when it opens and type /exit to come back. It adds graphic cards, captions and hook ideas. A kit with scheduling asks this later, when setting up your posts.
  3. It makes a spoken sample on your Mac, reads it locally and renders a short test reel. Wait for Editing ready (release …). The result and test reel are in ~/Reels/install-test/.

If your Mac says the file "can't be opened" or "Apple could not verify" it:

  1. Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security.
  2. Scroll down to the message about Install Reel Editor.command and click Open Anyway.
  3. Confirm and open the installer again. The installer itself needs no password.

Terminal fallback: replace <kit folder> with the full path to this unzipped kit folder (you can drag the folder from Finder into Terminal to see its path). Keep both the Brand folder and release.json from this kit:

bash "<kit folder>/install.sh" --brand "<kit folder>/Brand" --release "<kit folder>/release.json" --studio

No Homebrew, Apple command line tools or administrator account is needed. The installer never asks for a password. If you see a request to install developer tools or Homebrew, stop and use the installer bundled in this kit.

Fixes and waiting-reel alerts

Your client kit checks for fixes every week on this Mac. It keeps your settings, voice, brand guide, dictionary and look. It installs packages only when the package files changed, checks the update without contacting a service, and returns to the previous version if the check fails. You get a notification when something changed or needs attention. Updates pause while the code folder has unsaved changes. Re-running the installer keeps one update job.

The Reel Editor kit also checks the drop folders three times a day for reels waiting over a day. A Mac notification and ~/.reel/maintenance/STATUS.txt tell you what needs attention. Update notes are in ~/.reel/maintenance/updates.log.

2. Make a reel

  1. Drop a clip into one of the Raw folders in Desktop/Reels. The folder decides what you get.
  2. Wait for the notification. A 1-minute clip takes about 3 minutes.
  3. Open the new folder in Desktop/Reels, named by date and number.

Generated carousels use Codex: say yes when the installer offers it, and sign in with your ChatGPT account. Codex has usage limits; when it hits one, a carousel leaves a note to drop it again. Free B-roll uses Codex to pick matching moments and Claude Code to check the clips; sign in to both.

B-roll: use your own clips and free video

Raw - Reel with B-roll tries your own approved clips first, then free stock video. If no real moving clip fits what you say, you stay on screen. It never fills the gap with a still picture.

To add your own clips, film 6 to 10 seconds, with the action in the middle and no faces. Name each file subject - action.mov, such as coffee - pours into a mug.mov, and drop it in Desktop/Reels/Raw - My B-roll clips. A short result note appears beside it: approved, or rejected with the reason. Your original is kept there.

Open Desktop/Reels/B-roll shot list.html for ideas. Print it from your browser and check off each shot as you film.

For more free stock, add your own optional Pixabay key (free account; your key appears on that page when logged in) or Pexels key (new sign-ups may be paused) when installing. Everyone uses their own key. Re-running the installer keeps keys you already saved.

3. Make it yours

  • Your words: open ~/.reel/dictionary.json and add your program and framework names under terms. They get highlighted and spelled right. People and company names go under names: spelled right, not highlighted.
  • Your caption voice: edit ~/.reel/voice.md with who you talk to, words you use, and words you never use.
  • A call-to-action: add export REEL_WATCH_CTA="Comment YOURWORD" inside the reel-editor block in ~/.zshrc.
  • Change your look: double click the installer again and pick another option.

Your reels and posts

Your kit makes and schedules your reels and posts through your own Metricool. Nobody has to check the carousels or statics. Use Desktop/Reels for everything. Add photos to Photos. Film, drop a clip in a Raw folder, watch the finished reel, then drag it to Publish. Carousels and statics are made and scheduled by the Claude app at 6 AM and noon. Keep your Mac on and the Claude app open.

You need, on top of the above: your own Claude account (Pro or Max), your own Metricool account with your Instagram connected, and the Mac switched on. The Claude app makes and schedules carousels and statics at 6 AM and noon. The Mac must be on and the Claude app open.

Your daily safety net

Three times a day the studio checks whether the daily run finished, Claude and Metricool are connected, a Metricool post failed, or a reel has waited over three hours in Publish. A Mac notification and STATUS.txt at the top of your studio tell you what to do. The installer offers an optional health ping URL: use your own healthchecks.io HTTPS address, or press Return to skip. It sends only a check in (with /fail on problems), never your content. Nothing goes to an address you did not choose.

Normal reels plus Trial Reels

New studios use reels.trial: "both": a normal reel plus a separate Instagram Trial Reel copy. The trial never uses up a normal reel day. Both keep at least two hours from other posts. Existing settings stay as they are; true means trial only, false means normal only. Other connected networks keep the normal copy.

Optional daily story games

Stories start OFF. Write your own Desktop/Reels/Brand/brand-guide.md, then set stories.enabled to true in studio.json. The default stories.slots are ["08:00", "11:00", "17:00"]. Your colors, fonts and voice become vertical story frames: spot what's wrong, pick A/B, or keep/kill a note. Viewers reply with their pick; these are not poll stickers. They schedule through your Metricool as Instagram stories and never take a feed slot. They refuse to run while the guide says NOT FILLED IN YET.

Sunday filming pack

Each Sunday's daily run writes Filming packs/<Monday> filming pack.md: six prompts from your guide, each with a short opening, three beats and a last line for 25 to 45 seconds, plus three B-roll ideas for 5 to 10 seconds. Recent angles are avoided. Story prompts ask questions so you supply your real experience. The same filming box reminds you: phone at eye level phone, window light, one take, drop into a Raw folder in Desktop/Reels, watch the finished reel, then drag it into Publish. An unfinished guide skips the pack and leaves a note in STATUS.txt. We never invent your brand or your results.

What the Claude app does at 6 AM and noon

  1. At 6 AM and noon the Claude app looks at the next three days and finds every posting time that has no post yet (one you scheduled yourself in Metricool counts).
  2. For each, it picks the job by your brand guide's pillar mix, writes the post in your voice from your guide, picks photos you haven't used (matching each photo to its slide), and sets the slides in your colors and fonts.
  3. It puts a copy in Made and schedules it in your Metricool. A post that breaks one of your rules (a word from your never-say list) goes in as a draft with the reason instead of posting.
  4. When something fails you get a Mac notification; the post waits in Made with Not posted.txt, and an uncertain result waits for a schedule check before it can be marked done.

AI label: any post with a picture made by AI carries Instagram's "made with AI" label, always. Your real photos are never labelled. Set "ai": {"label": "always"} in studio.json to label every post.

Pictures made by AI (for slides with no photo left) need Codex, and use your own accounts. Without generated pictures, the studio uses only your photos and your guide.

Remove it

In Terminal, run ~/Ai/reel-editor/scripts/install-watcher.sh --uninstall to turn off the drop folder. Then delete the folders ~/Ai, ~/.reel and ~/.reel-venv, and the lines between # >>> reel-editor >>> and # <<< reel-editor <<< in ~/.zshrc. Your reels in Desktop/Reels stay.
